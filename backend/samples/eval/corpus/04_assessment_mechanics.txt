CS 401 Machine Learning — How Assessments Are Marked
====================================================

This document explains the marking conventions used for every assessed
component. It is written so that a student can predict the mark for their own
work before submitting it.


1. Examinations
---------------

Examinations are marked out of 100 and then scaled to the weight of the
component. The final examination and the midterm examination are both
hand-marked.

Partial credit is awarded generously on the final examination. A question
worth 10 points may earn between 0 and 10. There is no negative marking
anywhere in this course: incorrect work earns zero for that part and nothing
less. If you demonstrate a correct method with a wrong final number, the
method marks are still awarded.

The final examination has a short reading time of ten minutes before writing
begins, which is not counted. You may not begin writing during the reading
time.

Answers to examination questions are marked anonymously. Do not write your
name on the script. Scripts with a name on them are held for administrative
processing and may be returned to you after release of grades, which means you
cannot see your mark on the day of release.


2. Homework assignments
-----------------------

Each homework assignment is worth 10 points, and the ten assignments together
form the homework component, which is averaged and then weighted at 20
percent of the course grade.

Homework marks are published within one week of the submission deadline.
The published breakdown shows the points for each part, so partial credit is
visible. Code is marked on correctness first and style second; a correct
answer written badly still earns most of the marks, and a beautifully written
incorrect answer earns few.

Test cases provided in the assignment are worth roughly half the marks. Hidden
test cases, run only after the deadline, are worth the other half. This means
code that passes only the provided cases can score about 5 of 10.


3. Marking the project
---------------------

The project is marked against the rubric published in the course site, using
the four milestones described in the project document. Each milestone is
marked out of 25.

The four team members receive the same project mark. There is no mechanism
for individual marks within a team, and requests to differentiate team members
cannot be accommodated. If a team member contributes substantially less than
the others, the remedy is to tell the instructor before milestone 3, not after
the mark is released.


4. Regrade requests
-------------------

A regrade request must be submitted within seven days of the relevant result
being posted, using the regrade form in the course site. The form requires
you to identify the specific part you are disputing and to say what the
correct answer should be. "I do not agree with this mark" is not sufficient
and will be rejected.

Regrade requests are decided by the teaching assistant who set the work, or by
the instructor for the examinations. You will receive a decision within five
working days. A regrade may lower a mark as well as raise it, so consider
carefully before submitting.

Regrades of the final examination follow the university appeals process
rather than the course process, and the deadline for that is set by the
registry rather than by the course.


5. Academic conduct during assessments
--------------------------------------

All assessments in this course are individual unless explicitly described as
a project. Talking to another student during an examination is misconduct, as
is using a phone or an online resource during a sitting. Devices must be
powered off and stored away.

The midterm is conducted under the same rules as the final. There is no
permitted resource for either sitting, and the formula sheet is not provided
because the course expects you to know the material.
