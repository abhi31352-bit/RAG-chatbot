CS 401 Machine Learning — Project Milestones and Deadlines
===========================================================

The term project is worth 30 percent of the course grade. It is delivered in
four milestones, each with a hard date. This document gives the full schedule,
what is due when, and what happens if a milestone is missed.


1. Project structure
--------------------

The project is a four-person team building a machine learning system. Teams
are formed in week 2 and may not change after week 3. Each member must
contribute and each member receives the same project mark; there is no
individual project grade.

The project is graded out of 100 points across four milestones, worth 25
points each. There is no late penalty applied to milestones. A milestone that
is not submitted by its date scores zero for that milestone. This is a
deliberate design choice: partial credit is granted within a milestone, but a
missed milestone is a missed milestone.


2. Milestone schedule
---------------------

  Milestone 1 — Topic proposal and literature review
    Due: end of week 5
    Points: 25
    A one-page proposal naming the problem, the dataset you intend to use, and
    three papers whose techniques you plan to adapt. Also a short risk
    assessment listing what you think is the hardest part.

  Milestone 2 — Data pipeline and baseline
    Due: end of week 8
    Points: 25
    Working code that loads your dataset, cleans it, and produces a feature
    representation, plus a baseline model with a reported score on a held-out
    split. The baseline is expected to be simple.

  Milestone 3 — Model and evaluation
    Due: end of week 11
    Points: 25
    Your proposed model, trained and evaluated. You must report at least three
    metrics, a confusion matrix, and an ablation showing what each component
    contributes. A single accuracy number is not sufficient.

  Milestone 4 — Write-up and presentation
    Due: week 13 lecture, end of week 14
    Points: 25
    A report of at most 4000 words, a ten-minute presentation, and a
    reproducible script that regenerates every number in the report from the
    raw data.


3. Late submission and slippage
-------------------------------

Milestones have hard dates and no late penalty, but a slipping team can ask
for an extension once per project. Extensions are approved by the instructor
only, must be requested before the milestone date, and are granted at most
once for the whole project. A team that has used its extension and then
misses another milestone scores zero for that milestone.

Peer review of two other teams' milestone 3 submissions is due at the same
time as milestone 4. Peer review is worth 10 of the 100 project points, which
is separate from the four milestones worth 25 each.


4. Submission mechanics
-----------------------

Submissions go through the course site, not by email. Each milestone has a
single submission slot that closes at 23:59 local time on the date shown. The
slot does not reopen. Late slots are opened for appeals only.

Each submission must include a single archive containing the report, the code,
a requirements file pinning your dependencies, and a README giving the exact
commands needed to reproduce your reported numbers. A submission that cannot
be reproduced from its own README is marked down under milestone 4, because
reproducibility is part of what milestone 4 assesses.

Submitted work must be your own. See the academic integrity policy document
for what is permitted when using external code or assistance.


5. Presentation scheduling
--------------------------

Each team presents during the week 13 lecture slot allocated to it. The slot
allocation is published in the course site. Teams that miss their allocated
slot present in the catch-up session in week 14, and a catch-up presentation
receives no separate credit.
